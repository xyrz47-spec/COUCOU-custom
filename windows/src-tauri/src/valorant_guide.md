# Valorant coaching guide

This guide is general convention that strong players and coaches commonly teach. It is not data learned from any
particular player's games, and it does not replace the footage: use it to judge what the player did against what is
commonly considered sound, and say so as a convention, never as a certainty. Agent kits, maps and numbers change with
patches; where a rule depends on them, say it depends.

Each section has an id in brackets. A review moment cites the id of the section it is judged against.

## Information and decisions

### [INFO-1] Decide with the information you had
Judge a decision by what the player could know at that time: the sound heard, the minimap, the teammates alive, the
timer, the economy. A choice that failed but was reasonable with that information is not an error; a choice that
worked but ignored clear information is still a habit to fix.

### [INFO-2] Information before commitment
Strong players gather information with utility, sound or a teammate's call before they put their body in a fight they
cannot trade. Entering an unknown space with no information and no trade is a coin flip the player lost on average.

### [INFO-3] Update the plan on new information
When the first contact shows the enemy's plan (a stack, a rotation, a fake), the round plan changes. Continuing the
original plan after clear contrary information is a common mid-round mistake.

### [INFO-4] Do not assume what the footage does not show
Where an enemy was, what a teammate said in voice, or what an unseen utility did cannot be stated as fact. Say what
cannot be known, and judge only the part that can be seen.

## Aim and gunplay

### [AIM-1] Crosshair placement
Keep the crosshair at head height and on the angle you expect an enemy to appear, so that the first correction is
small. Looking at the floor or the sky while moving, or placing the crosshair on a corner's edge instead of where the
head will be, costs the first shot.

### [AIM-2] Stop before you shoot
Accuracy with most weapons needs the player to be nearly still when firing. Shooting while strafing at range, or
firing before the movement has settled, is a frequent cause of lost duels. Counter-strafing is the usual fix.

### [AIM-3] Spray control and burst discipline
At range, short bursts or taps beat a full spray; at close range a controlled spray is fine. Holding the trigger past
the weapon's accurate spread at range wastes the duel.

### [AIM-4] Take fights with an advantage
Prefer duels where you are the one peeking with information, or the one holding a tight angle from cover. A wide
open swing against a held angle gives the defender the first shot.

### [AIM-5] Peek type matters
A shoulder peek baits a shot, a wide peek gives information and speed, a jiggle peek gives sound and a look. Using a
peek with no purpose, or the same peek repeatedly against an awake enemy, makes the player predictable.

### [AIM-6] Reload and swap discipline
Reloading or swapping after a duel, out of sight, and not in the open, avoids dying with an empty weapon. A reload
started in a space an enemy can still reach is a risk.

## Movement and positioning

### [POS-1] Use cover and fight from it
Fight from where only one enemy can see you. Standing in the open between two angles invites a trade the other way.

### [POS-2] Do not fight two angles at once
If a position is exposed to two enemies, move so that you only face one at a time, or clear one with utility first.

### [POS-3] Make noise on purpose
Running makes sound; walking or standing still hides it. Running past enemies you want to ambush, or walking when
speed matters, are both mistakes of timing.

### [POS-4] Reposition after a kill
After a kill or a shot that revealed your position, the enemy team knows where you were. Strong players change position
or fall back rather than hold the same spot for the next fight.

### [POS-5] Off-angles and distance
An unexpected position or a longer range than the enemy expects can win a duel, but only when it is still covered by a
teammate or an escape. Isolated off-angles with no support lose rounds when they fail.

### [POS-6] Do not be the last to know
Staying near the group so you can trade and hear calls usually beats being far away with no information. An isolated
player dies without trade more often.

## Teamwork

### [TEAM-1] Trading
A trade means a teammate can kill the enemy who killed you within a short time. Entering ahead of the group, or
leaving a teammate to fight alone, removes the trade and costs a 4v5 or worse.

### [TEAM-2] Spacing
Stay close enough to trade, far enough not to be hit by the same utility or the same angle. Stacked on the same
pixel is as bad as being far away.

### [TEAM-3] Communicate what matters
Short, useful calls: where, how many, what utility, health. Silence about a clearly seen enemy, or long talk during a
fight, both cost the team. The footage cannot show voice: do not claim what was or was not said.

### [TEAM-4] Use utility for teammates
Flashes, smokes and scans are most valuable when timed to a teammate's swing. Using a flash for yourself when a
teammate is the one entering, or throwing it with no one to follow, wastes it.

### [TEAM-5] Take space together
Taking a site or a space works when the group commits at the same time with utility, not one at a time.

## Utility and abilities

### [UTIL-1] Use utility to enable, not to remember
Abilities earn their value by creating an advantage at a specific time. Dying with unused utility is a common mistake;
so is using it early with no follow-up.

### [UTIL-2] Timing of flashes and smokes
A flash is best when the player or a teammate swings immediately after it. A smoke is best when it blocks the angle the
enemy would use, and when the team uses the time it buys. Check whether the footage shows a follow-up.

### [UTIL-3] Hold a recon or info ability when it is the only one
Using the single information ability on a guess, then having nothing for the real contact, is often worse than keeping
it for the moment of need. This depends on the agent; say so.

### [UTIL-4] Defensive utility is for timing and delay
A defender's utility is usually best used to slow, to give a warning, or to delay a push, not to stop an attack
completely. Using it too early leaves nothing for the second wave.

### [UTIL-5] Alternatives to using utility
When the footage shows an ability used, say what the alternative was: keep it, use it at another angle, use it for a
teammate, or use it later. Do not name a better use that depends on an ability the agent does not have.

### [UTIL-6] Ultimate usage
Ultimates swing rounds when they are used with a plan and a team follow-up, not when they are used out of habit or too
late. Holding a ready ultimate for several rounds with no reason, or dying with it, is a loss of value.

## Economy

### [ECON-1] Buy as a team
A round where some players buy full and others save splits the team's power. Strong teams agree on a full buy, a force,
a half buy or a save, and follow it.

### [ECON-2] Save when the round is lost
Saving a weapon after a round is clearly lost keeps the next round's economy. Taking a pointless fight after the round
is decided costs a weapon.

### [ECON-3] Do not drop below the next buy
Spending so that the next round cannot be a full buy, when a full buy was possible, is an economy mistake unless the
force was a deliberate decision.

### [ECON-4] Armor and abilities count
A weapon with no armor, or a full weapon with no abilities, is a weaker buy than it looks. Consider the whole kit.

### [ECON-5] Eco rounds have a job
On an eco round the plan is to get a weapon, deal damage, or get information, not to take a fight that cannot be won.
The footage should show which one the player was attempting.

## Round structure and time

### [ROUND-1] Have a plan for the round
Attackers need a plan: a site, a split, a default. Defenders need a setup: where each player is, what is held, what
is a retake. A round with no visible plan is a common cause of losing without a fight.

### [ROUND-2] Use the timer
On attack, spending the timer in the wrong place leaves too little time to plant or to use utility. On defense, the
timer is on your side after a plant fails, and late fights are not necessary.

### [ROUND-3] The first kill
The first kill of a round is worth a large share of the round. A first fight taken with information and a trade is
worth more than one taken alone. Look at who took it and under what conditions.

### [ROUND-4] Defaulting
A default is a way to gather information and get map control without committing. Rushing a site with no information
may work as a surprise, but it is a gamble; say which one it was.

### [ROUND-5] Anti-eco and force rounds
Against a weaker buy, play safe and keep the group together; the risk is giving an easy kill to a cheap weapon by
peeking alone.

## Attacking

### [ATK-1] Take map control before the site
Taking space with utility and information before going onto the site makes the site entry easier. Entering the site
directly from spawn with no control is only sound against a prepared plan.

### [ATK-2] Entry with support
The entry player is the one who takes the first fight; support flashes and follows. An entry with no support is
usually a lost trade.

### [ATK-3] Plant for the post-plant
Plant where you can defend the bomb and where teammates have lines to the defusing player. A plant that leaves the
team with no angle on the defuse is a weak plant.

### [ATK-4] Fakes and pressure
A fake is meant to pull defenders away. A fake with no real action behind it, or a real execute that ignores what the
fake showed, wastes the idea.

## Post-plant

### [POST-1] Play for the timer
After a plant, the bomb is the attackers' advantage. Taking a fight in the open for the defuse is usually worse than
holding an angle and letting the timer work.

### [POST-2] Separate angles
Attackers hold different angles on the defuse so one utility does not clear them all. Standing together in front of
the defuse is a mistake.

### [POST-3] Use utility on the defuser
Utility on the bomb, to deny the defuse or to damage the defender, is stronger than hoping for a peek. Check whether it
was used and when.

### [POST-4] Do not run into the defender
When the defender must come to you, let them. Leaving a good angle to hunt the defender gives away the advantage.

## Defending and retakes

### [DEF-1] Hold to delay, not to win every fight
On defense, the goal is often to trade one for one, get information, and fall back to a retake with the team alive.
A defender who dies alone for no information has made the retake harder.

### [DEF-2] Rotate on information
Rotating on one sound or one kill without confirming it, or staying on a site that is clearly not being attacked, are
both mistakes. Rotation needs a reason.

### [DEF-3] Retake with the team
A retake works with the group arriving together with utility. Retaking one at a time is a common failure.

### [DEF-4] Retake order
Clear the near angles first, use utility to get a position, then take the bomb. Going straight to the bomb with
unclear angles is a typical retake loss.

### [DEF-5] Know when to save
A retake with too few players or too little time is lost; saving the weapon is the better call. Make that decision
early rather than at the last second.

## Clutches and late-round

### [CLUTCH-1] Time and isolation
In a clutch, the timer and separation are the main tools. Splitting the enemies with sound or utility and taking them
one at a time is stronger than taking a group together.

### [CLUTCH-2] Do not rush a 1vN
Rushing into a group is rarely the best decision. If the bomb is not planted and you are the defender, time is often
on your side; if you are the attacker, plant or use the time to create angles.

### [CLUTCH-3] Use sound
Use sound to bait, to hide or to place enemies. Running loudly in a clutch gives away the position the player would
rather hide.

### [CLUTCH-4] Stay off the same repeated spot
In a 1vN, change the angle between kills; the remaining enemies know where the last kill happened.

## Mental and habits

### [HABIT-1] Reset after a mistake
One lost round or fight should not change the next decision. Tilt shows as rushed fights and ignored plans.

### [HABIT-2] Repeat what works, change what does not
A pattern that failed several rounds in a row with the same result (the same peek, the same entry) should change.
Note repeats across the match, and across matches if the data is given.

### [HABIT-3] Review one thing at a time
Practice one habit per session. A review that lists many issues helps less than naming one to three and a drill for
each.

### [HABIT-4] Deliberate practice
Pair every issue with a way to practise it: a range drill, a custom game, or a rule for the next match (for example:
"do not peek without a trade for the first ten rounds").
